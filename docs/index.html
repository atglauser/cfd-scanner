    Use it as a shortlist, not a decision

    The scan narrows about 60 markets down to a few worth opening on your own chart. Most costly mistakes happen after that step, when the order is placed. This page is the routine and the guard rails.

    Daily routine (about 15 minutes)

    #DoWhy
    1Set your account size, risk per trade and max total open risk at the top of the Scan tab. They are remembered in this browser.The defaults (1,000 CHF, 2%, 4%) are examples. Every size on the page follows these three numbers.
    2Check the status badges: a green age and (nearly) all markets downloaded.An old or partial scan means stale levels.
    3Read the Before you place anything box first.It tells you how many positions your cap allows and which setups are really the same bet.
    4Pick at most the number of positions your cap allows (2 at 2% / 4%), from different currencies or themes.Correlated positions lose together.
    5Open each one on your broker's chart and rebuild entry, stop and target at the broker's prices.Yahoo quotes are not CFD prices. Some CFDs, oil especially, trade at a fixed offset.

    Costly mistakes to avoid

    MistakeHow it happensGuard
    OversizingPicking a round number of units instead of computing them. Ten units where four fit can double or triple the planned loss.Compute the size from the stop every time. Never size from "what feels right" or from margin available.
    Copying levels blindlyEntering at Yahoo prices on a CFD that trades at an offset.Shift every level to your broker's price before placing the order.
    Stacking correlated tradesLong USDCHF, long GBPCHF and short CHFJPY look like three trades but are one bet against the franc.Follow the correlation warning: take one.
    ChasingThe limit order did not fill, so you enter at market.No fill, no trade. The setup only exists at its entry price.
    Moving the stopWidening it as price approaches.Never widen. Tighten or close early if the picture changes.

    Forcing a small account into big contractsIndex CFDs at minimum size can risk many times the budget.Only trade setups from the ranked table, never from Not tradable at your risk.

    What to expect

    • The ranking is unproven. It has not been backtested. Start with paper trading or minimum size for 4 to 8 weeks and keep a journal: setup, filled or not, result, and whether you followed the plan.
    • Small accounts are mostly costs. With a small risk budget, spreads and minimum sizes rule out many markets. That is not a bug; it is what the arithmetic says.
    • Losing streaks are normal. Even at a 2:1 reward-to-risk, 5 to 8 losses in a row happen. At 2% per trade that is a 10 to 16% drawdown. At double the size it is a third of the account.
    • Fewer trades is usually better. Some days nothing survives the chart check. Doing nothing is a valid outcome.

    Hard rules

    • Never risk more than your per-trade budget, and never exceed your total open-risk cap.
    • Every order has a stop when it is placed, not later.
    • Set a drawdown limit in advance (for example −20% of the starting account). When it is hit, stop trading and review the journal before continuing.

    Not investment advice. CFDs are leveraged products and most retail accounts lose money trading them.

    What this is

    A once-a-day technical scan of about 60 CFD markets: FX pairs, stock indices, energy, metals, US Treasury futures and crypto. For each market it asks three questions: is there a trend, where would a low-risk entry be, and can this account afford the trade after costs. It then ranks the answers.

    It is a filter, not a signal service. It narrows 60 charts down to a handful worth opening yourself.

    1. DataDaily (2y) and hourly (180d) prices
    2. IndicatorsEMA, RSI, ATR on 1D / 4H / 1H
    3. TrendWeighted vote across timeframes
    4. Levels4H swing highs and lows
    5. SetupEntry, stop, target
    6. SizingUnits from your risk, after costs
    7. RankingScore with penalties

    Indicators

    IndicatorWhat it measuresHow the scan uses it
    EMA 20Short-term average price, weighted to recent barsThe "value zone" in a trend: entries are placed at the 4H EMA20, where pullbacks often pause.
    EMA 50Medium-term averagePrice above EMA20 above EMA50 means the short and medium trend agree.
    EMA 200Long-term average, widely watchedConfirms the trend on 1D and 4H. Ignored on 1H, where 200 bars is only about 8 trading days.
    RSI 14Momentum, 0 to 100Not used to find trades. Used as a brake: buying when RSI is very high, or selling when it is very low, often means chasing an extended move.
    ATR 14Average bar range: how much the market normally movesThe ruler for everything: stop distance, how far the entry is from price, and how close two levels must be to merge.
    Swing levelsRecent 4H highs and lows where price turnedSupport and resistance: used for targets, for placing stops beyond them, and to strengthen entries.

    Why these: they are simple, standard and widely watched, so they are easy to check on any chart. More exotic indicators would add parameters to tune without clear evidence of a better result.

    Trend: a weighted vote across timeframes

    Each timeframe scores +1 (up), −1 (down) or 0 (mixed):

    • Up when close > EMA20 > EMA50, and on 1D and 4H also EMA50 > EMA200 or close > EMA200. Down is the mirror image.
    • Weights: 1D × 2, 4H × 1.5, 1H × 1. The weighted average runs from −1 to +1.
    • At +0.5 or above the bias is LONG; at −0.5 or below it is SHORT; otherwise the market goes to the No trend table.

    Why: trend-following works best when timeframes agree. The daily chart gets the most weight because it reflects the larger flows; the hourly chart only confirms timing. In practice at least two timeframes must agree; if the third points the other way, the two must be the daily and the 4H.

    Support and resistance

    • A swing high is a 4H bar whose high is the highest of the 3 bars on each side; a swing low is the mirror image. The scan looks back 180 4H bars (about 30 trading days), plus the highest and lowest point of that window.
    • Levels closer than 0.5 × ATR(4H) are merged into one, so a zone counts once.
    • The nearest level above price is shown as resistance, the nearest below as support.

    Why: places where price turned before are where orders tend to sit again. They make natural targets, and a stop placed just beyond one is less likely to be hit by ordinary noise.

    The setup: entry, stop, target

    Rule (LONG; SHORT is the mirror)Rationale
    EntryBUY LIMIT at the 4H EMA20 if price is above it. If a support level is within 0.5 ATR of the EMA, the entry is the average of the two. If price is already below the EMA, BUY MARKET.Buy pullbacks in an uptrend rather than breakouts: a better price and a shorter stop for the same idea.
    Stop1 × ATR(4H) below entry. If a support level sits within 1.5 ATR below entry, the stop goes 0.25 ATR beyond that level when that is further away.Far enough to survive normal noise, and never just in front of a level where stops cluster.
    TargetThe next resistance at least 0.5 ATR above entry, minus 0.1 ATR. If there is none, 2 × the risk (2R).Take profit just in front of where sellers are likely to appear, instead of hoping for a break.

    The scan shows how far the limit entry is from current price, in ATR. Beyond 1.5 ATR the order may never fill, and the score is reduced.

    Sizing: risk first, after costs

    Position size comes from the amount you are willing to lose, not from how much the account can buy:

    • Budget = account × risk per trade (e.g. 1,000 CHF × 2% = 20 CHF).
    • Risk per unit = (stop distance + spread) × currency rate to CHF + overnight financing per unit for 3 days.
    • Units = budget ÷ risk per unit, rounded down to the broker's size step.
    • If even the minimum size would risk more than the budget, the setup goes to Not tradable at your risk. This is common with stock indices on small accounts.
    • Net reward = gross move to target minus spread and financing. Net R:R = net reward ÷ total risk.
    • Margin = notional value × the broker's margin rate. It shows how much of the account the position locks up.

    Why: on a small account, costs and minimum sizes decide what is tradable. A setup with an attractive gross R:R can be poor once a wide spread is paid on a tight stop. Rounding down means the real risk is at or below the budget, never above.

    Score and ranking

    Score = |trend strength| × net R:R (R:R capped at 4, so one lucky distant target cannot dominate), then reduced:

    IssuePenaltyWhy
    Entry far from priceNone up to 1.5 ATR, then gradually down to × 0.3 at 3 ATRA limit order that never fills is not a trade.
    RSI extreme against the tradeGradual, up to × 0.4. Shorts: daily RSI 35 → 25 or 4H 30 → 20. Longs: daily 65 → 75 or 4H 70 → 80.Stretched moves tend to snap back before continuing.
    Net R:R below 1.5× 0.5With realistic win rates, low R:R needs a very high hit rate to pay.
    Fees above 10% of reward× 0.5Costs are certain; the reward is not.
    Margin above 50% of account× 0.5One position should not lock up most of the account.
    Below minimum sizeScore 0, listed separatelyNot executable within the risk rule.

    Penalties are gradual rather than on/off, so a reading of 30.4 is not treated very differently from 29.9.

    Risk rules built in

    • Risk per trade: default 2% of the account, set at the top of the Scan tab.
    • Total open risk cap: default 4%. The dashboard adds up the top 5 setups and warns when they exceed the cap, with the maximum number of positions you can hold.
    • Correlation: FX setups that bet on the same currency in the same direction (for example long USDCHF and long GBPCHF are both short CHF) are flagged. Treat them as one trade.
    • Costs: skip setups where spread + financing take more than 10% of the reward.

    Limitations

    • Not backtested. The rules are standard trend-following logic, but this exact combination has not been tested on historical data. There is no evidence yet that the ranking predicts outcomes. Daily scans are archived in data/history so the setups can be checked later.
    • Not a broker feed. Prices are Yahoo Finance futures, index, spot and FX quotes. CFD prices can sit at an offset (oil CFDs often track a different contract month), so levels must be checked against your own platform.
    • Contract specs are estimates. Spreads, minimum sizes, steps, margin rates and financing are generic retail CFD values. Real spreads widen at night, around news and on illiquid pairs.
    • Once a day. The scan runs on weekday mornings (UTC). By the afternoon a setup can be filled, invalidated or gone. Nothing is scanned on weekends, although crypto trades.
    • No news or calendar. Central bank decisions, CPI, payrolls, oil inventories and elections are not considered, and they can move price through any stop.
    • Gaps and slippage. Stops are not guaranteed. Over a weekend or a news event, the loss can exceed the planned risk.
    • Trend-following has losing streaks. It works in trends and loses in ranges. A string of small losses is the expected cost of catching the few large moves.
    • Correlation is partial. Only shared FX currencies are detected. Links such as US indices with each other, oil with CAD, or gold with real yields are not flagged.
    • Simple levels. Swing levels only look back about 30 trading days on the 4H chart. Older or weekly levels are not considered.
